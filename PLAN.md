## Product purpose and requirements

Build an offline-first native grocery app for couples and families, with iOS and Android distribution and a backend on DigitalOcean. Keep the shopping experience simple while making persistence, collaboration and recovery reliable.

- Support multiple households and more than two members in the data model.
- Maintain one underlying shopping list with common and grouped-by-responsible-person views, including unassigned items.
- Track creator, assigned buyer and actual purchaser separately.
- Preserve independent purchase actions, including two people buying the same item. Retrying one operation must not create another purchase.
- Support item creation, editing, deletion/restoration, optional quantities and units, assignment, purchasing and purchase reversal.
- Persist offline changes across app termination and synchronize on reconnection under explicitly approved conflict rules.
- Enforce household authorization, secure invitations and account deletion.
- An account is required before using shopping lists. No anonymous/guest list mode in v1. Offline use after initial authentication remains required; session expiry and offline access rules need design.
- Keep synchronization semantics independent of transport for possible future nearby transfer.

Out of scope for v1: nearby device discovery/transfer, payments, subscriptions, ads, AI, recipes, meal planning, barcode scanning, price comparison, advanced analytics, Kafka, Redis, Kubernetes and microservices. Do not ship a nonfunctional Sync Nearby button.

## General household sharing rule

Approved on 2026-11-19: anything created by any member within a household is available to the entire household unless explicitly specified otherwise. Apply this default across household content, including lists, items and custom units; do not ask for separate sharing approval for each new content type.

Any current household member may edit or remove household content created by another member unless explicitly restricted. Apply this collaborative default to lists, items and custom-unit management without asking separately for each content type. Preserve attribution/history and the approved conflict rules; permission to remove does not define permanent data erasure.

These rules are household-scoped, not cross-household/public sharing, and do not automatically grant account, membership or security-administration privileges. Preserve explicit exceptions, including notifications directed only to the involved people. Anonymous/private use without an account was rejected.

## Approach and rationale

Agreed planning direction: define purchase behavior and synchronization semantics first, then build an offline mobile slice, identity/households, cloud sync, infrastructure and release preparation.

The alternative considered was an early two-device shared slice. That would reveal integration issues earlier but require authentication and backend work before validating the shopping experience. The offline-first sequence was recommended and the user agreed to the sequence. Move semantic design ahead of local data-model implementation to avoid costly schema changes later.

### Synchronization conflict matrix

- Independent additions, including identical or similar names.
- Retry of one purchase operation versus two distinct purchases.
- Concurrent edits to the same item.
- Concurrent assignment to different members.
- Delete/edit, delete/purchase and restore/delete conflicts.
- Reversal arriving before the referenced purchase.
- Out-of-order delivery and inconsistent device clocks.
- Account/session changes with pending offline edits, without exposing or attaching one account's data to another account.
- Multiple households and membership revoked while offline.
- App termination or network changes during upload/download.

Operation IDs alone do not establish convergence. Do not rely solely on client wall-clock timestamps. The server derives actor identity from authenticated credentials and membership, never from an untrusted actor ID. Changes and pending operations must commit atomically locally; accepted operations and server projections must commit transactionally.

## End-to-end release acceptance scenario

Two authenticated members join a household. A adds milk and assigns B. B sees the same item in common and grouped views. Both independently purchase it while offline, with further offline edits as specified by the approved rules. After reconnection both devices converge and preserve both purchases. Replaying one operation creates no extra purchase. An unrelated user cannot read or mutate the household. Local changes survive restart. Backend HTTPS, backup restoration and native-device tests are verified before release.

## Approved purchase behavior

Confirmed in the planning conversation on 2026-11-18:

- Tapping Purchased defaults to the remaining requested quantity visible on the buyer's phone. If nothing has been purchased yet, this is the full requested amount.
- The buyer can manually change the purchase quantity.
- Adding an item without specifying a quantity defaults to 1; the unit is optional. Example: entering only “Milk” creates a requested quantity of 1, which can be edited.
- Example: milk requests 2 cartons; 1 carton has already been purchased and is visible locally. The next Purchased action defaults to 1 carton.
- Independent purchases retain their separate quantities and buyer attribution, and their quantities add together, including when recorded offline. Explicitly reaffirmed by the user: one person buys 1 carton and another buys 3, so the purchased total is 4. Never apply the household's requested-quantity larger/smaller rule to distinct purchases or let one overwrite another. Retrying the same purchase operation does not add to the total again.
- Excess purchases leave the item completed, with the excess quantity and both buyers' indicators visible. Approved example: two offline buyers each purchase 2 cartons against a request for 2; after synchronization show “4 of 2 bought · 2 extra” and retain both purchase records.
- Household members may correct or undo their own purchases and other members' purchases within the same household. The user explicitly approved cross-member corrections and reversals. Preserve the original purchaser attribution and record who made the correction/reversal in history; do not silently erase the original purchase.

- Corrections and reversals automatically reopen an item when its effective purchased total falls below the requested quantity, showing the remaining amount. Example: 2 cartons requested and 2 purchased; correcting the purchase to 1 returns the item to the active list with 1 remaining. If the effective total still meets or exceeds the request, the item remains completed.

Offline implication: a phone may not yet know about another buyer's purchase, so both buyers can purchase the same remaining amount. Preserve both actual purchases; synchronization must not silently reduce their recorded quantities. The exact synchronization protocol remains unapproved.

Additional purchase, demand and quantity rules are recorded in the approved 2026-11-24 batch below. Technical reconciliation details remain open.

## Approved assignment behavior

- For a newly added item, the editor (the person adding it) is the default assignee. User decision: “by default editor is assignee”, in response to the new-item assignment question. This replaces the proposed unassigned default.
- Creator, assigned buyer and actual purchaser remain separate concepts; explicit assignment changes and unassigned items remain supported per the handover.
- Editing an existing item's name or quantity preserves its current assignee, even when another member makes the edit. Assignment changes only when explicitly changed.

## Approved deletion conflict behavior

- If one member deletes an item while another purchases it offline, the item remains visible in the list after synchronization, with the purchase retained and attributed normally.
- The purchased item uses normal item/purchase presentation. The earlier no-conflict-notice decision is superseded by the user's later requirement that all deletion conflicts notify the user; keeping a purchased item visible remains approved.
- When deletion conflicts with an offline name or quantity edit, deletion takes precedence: the item stays deleted. An ordinary edit does not bring it back.
- All deletion conflicts must notify both people involved, including delete/edit and delete/purchase conflicts, regardless of which action determines the final state. Do not broadcast these notices to uninvolved household members. Use both in-app notices and phone push notifications, including when the app is closed. This supersedes the proposed omission of push from v1. Grouping and acknowledgment behavior remain open. This does not require approval dialogs or notifications for ordinary non-conflicting deletions.
- Implementation requirement: keep in-app conflict notices available independently of push delivery or permission. Select the push provider, device-token lifecycle and delivery/deduplication design during technical planning; push is not authoritative synchronization state.
- Deliberately deleting an item after seeing its purchase removes it from the list. Purchased items are not permanently protected from deletion. Preserve purchase history as required by the handover.
- Distinguish an informed deletion from an offline deletion made without seeing the conflicting purchase. Exact causal/version metadata remains part of the unapproved synchronization design.
- Partial-purchase conflicts and a later reversal of the conflicting purchase still need clarification.

## Approved requested-quantity conflict behavior

- For concurrent edits to the same item's requested quantity, the larger amount wins by default. Example: milk requests 2 cartons; concurrent offline changes to 3 and 4 resolve to 4, not 7.
- The conflict-resolution rule is configurable through one shared household setting, applied consistently to that household's lists for all members. Approved options: “Larger amount wins” (default), “Smaller amount wins”, and “Ask us to choose”. Any current household member may change this setting. Handling policy changes while devices are offline remains unapproved; this permission does not establish permissions for other household settings.
- In “Ask us to choose” mode, conflicting quantities require a user choice. Until resolved, display and use the larger amount for remaining-quantity and purchase calculations, with a “Needs a choice” marker. Shopping can continue. Example: conflicting values 3 and 4 temporarily use 4. Any current household member may resolve the conflict, including members whose edits did not cause it.
- For competing manual resolutions of the same quantity conflict, the first valid resolution accepted by the server stands. Show the other person the accepted amount and that the conflict was already resolved; they may subsequently edit the item normally. An offline resolution remains pending until server acceptance. This rule is not a general first-write-wins policy for ordinary edits; exact protocol and handling newly arriving conflicting edits remain open.
- Quantity conflicts requiring a manual choice notify both people whose edits conflicted through in-app notices and push notifications. Other household members may see and resolve the marked item without receiving these conflict notices. Automatically resolved quantity conflicts (larger/smaller wins) stay quiet: show the resolved quantity normally, without in-app conflict notices or push alerts. All deletion conflicts still notify both involved people.
- This rule concerns conflicting requested-quantity edits. It does not establish a rule for purchase corrections, different units or names, or an ordinary subsequent edit made after seeing the current quantity. Do not apply a permanent maximum that prevents intentional reductions.

## Approved assignment conflict behavior

- Concurrent assignments of the same item to different buyers require a manual choice. Do not silently pick one buyer.
- Until resolved, show the item in the Unassigned section of the grouped view with a “Choose buyer” marker, also visible in the common view. Preserve both proposed assignments; this temporary display is not an explicit unassignment.
- Any current household member may resolve an assignment conflict. Both people whose assignment edits conflicted receive in-app and push notices; do not additionally notify uninvolved proposed buyers.
- The first valid assignment resolution accepted by the server stands. Other resolvers see the accepted choice and may subsequently reassign normally. Offline resolutions remain pending until accepted. These rules reuse the approved manual quantity-resolution workflow.

## Approved duplicate-addition behavior

- Automatically merge independently added items with matching names in the same shopping list. The user rejected the proposal to keep such additions separate.
- Requested quantities of matching additions follow the shared household quantity-conflict rule, rather than being summed. Under the default larger-amount rule, Milk ×2 and Milk ×3 merge into Milk ×3. The smaller/ask options apply when selected for the household.
- For automatic merging, match names and units after trimming outer whitespace, collapsing repeated spaces and ignoring capitalization. Merge only matching names with matching units, including when both units are omitted. Keep Milk vs Oat milk, litres vs cartons, and omitted vs specified units separate unless a separately approved user-confirmed duplicate flow applies. Do not infer unit conversions, automatic fuzzy merging or merging across lists/households.
- Before creating a new item, detect similar existing items and ask the user whether the proposed item is a duplicate. Similarity alone must not automatically merge or discard the new item. This user-approved similarity prompt extends the earlier exact-match-only planning scope; the exact-match automatic merge rule remains in effect. Local/offline operation must remain supported; no AI service dependency is implied.
- The possible-duplicate prompt offers “Use existing item” and “Create separately”. Example: Tomato versus Tomatoes. First check the household's existing product catalog in the database to avoid multiple similar product definitions, not just unfinished entries in the current list. The catalog is shared within that household only, not across unrelated households. Matching sensitivity, incompatible-unit handling and repeat-prompt behavior still need specification.
- Products are reusable household catalog entries, distinct from shopping-list entries representing current demand. Keep a product in the catalog after its list entry is purchased or removed; users select it again rather than recreate its definition each time. Reusing a known product must not merge demands across lists or carry historical purchases into a fresh demand. Apply the existing quantity/assignment merge rules only when eligible demands coincide in the same list. Exact catalog schema remains undesigned.
- Explicitly removing a product from the catalog retires it from future selections while preserving existing shopping-list entries and purchase history. This is distinct from removing one list entry.
- When someone enters a removed catalog product's name again, offer to restore its existing catalog entry instead of creating a duplicate product definition. Restoring the catalog product re-enables future selection; it does not restore old list demand or count historical purchases toward a new demand. Declining restoration, matching/unit details and offline retirement conflicts remain open.
- Selecting a saved catalog product prefills its usual measurement unit; the new requested quantity defaults to 1. Users may adjust the quantity and unit before adding. A unit override on a shopping-list entry does not change the catalog default. The usual unit changes only through explicit editing of the catalog product. Example: Milk defaults to litres; choosing cartons for one entry leaves litres as the default next time. Existing list entries and purchase history must not be silently reinterpreted. Initial default-unit setup remains to be specified.
- When merged additions have different assigned buyers, including each creator's default assignment, use the approved assignment-conflict workflow: temporarily Unassigned with “Choose buyer”, any household member may resolve, both involved editors receive in-app/push notices, and the first valid resolution accepted by the server stands. Matching assignments remain unchanged.
- Preserve both original addition records and credit both creators in the merged item's history, without requiring extra creator labels in the main list. Approved on 2026-11-19. Do not replace the original creators with a single arbitrary creator during merging.
- Preserve distinct actual purchase records and their attribution through a merge; replaying an operation must not create extra items or purchases. Stable identity/alias handling belongs in the synchronization design.
- Deliberately adding a product after seeing its previous list entry completed or deleted creates a fresh demand/list entry with no inherited purchases, reusing the existing catalog product. It does not create a duplicate product definition. Do not merge it into or reopen that old demand. This supports intentional repeat shopping; automatic list-entry matching applies to active shopping needs.
- Offline additions made without seeing completion/deletion still need causal rules. Do not decide merge eligibility solely from event arrival order.

## Approved measurement-unit behavior

Approved on 2026-11-19:

- Decimal quantities are allowed only for suitable measurement units. Cartons accept whole numbers only; kilograms and litres accept decimals. Apply the unit's quantity rules to requested amounts and recorded purchases.
- Users can create their own measurement units. A custom unit created by one member becomes available to all members of that household, with the same quantity rules.
- Custom-unit creation offers an optional choice between “Whole numbers only” and “Decimals allowed”. If skipped, the unit defaults to whole numbers only. Decimal support requires explicit selection.
- Cartons, kg and litres were examples, not an exhaustive catalog. The user requested a broad, sensible shopping-unit list and delegated selection of units.
- Proposed initial catalog: whole-number units — item/piece, pack, packet, box, carton, bottle, can, jar, bag, tub, tray, roll, loaf, bunch, head and pair; decimal-capable units — gram (g), kilogram (kg), milligram (mg), ounce by weight (oz), pound (lb), millilitre (ml), centilitre (cl), litre (l), fluid ounce (fl oz), pint, quart, gallon, centimetre (cm) and metre (m). These are planning selections, not individual user requirements. Keep weight ounces distinct from fluid ounces; label regional US/imperial volume variants explicitly if offered. Do not add conversion functionality implicitly.
- Any current household member may edit or remove custom units under the general collaborative-content rule. Removing a custom unit retires it from new selections while preserving its unit and quantity rules on existing items and purchase records. Do not erase, convert or invalidate those records as a result of unit removal.
- An item's unit may be changed before purchases or after all its purchases have been undone. While effective purchases remain, a different unit requires a fresh item. Historical purchases retain their original units; never reinterpret 2 cartons as 2 litres. See the approved batch for concurrent unit-change/purchase handling.
- Decimal-capable units support three decimal places for requested and purchased quantities; accept comma or dot as the decimal separator. Automatically round extra precision. Only when rounding a positive amount produces zero, ask for a larger amount or smaller unit instead of saving zero. Whole-number units and quantities without a unit remain integer-only. Exact tie rounding and ambiguous mixed-separator parsing require technical specification.
- Custom-unit matching, conflicts and offline retirement are covered in the approved batch below. Cross-household unit sharing remains unapproved.
- Existing automatic-merge rules still require matching units; custom units do not imply automatic conversion or equivalence to built-in units.

## Approved restoration behavior

- Restore brings back the same deleted item with its quantities, assignment and purchase history. Completion reflects effective purchases: a fully purchased item returns as completed unless a matching fresh active demand exists (see the exception below); a partially purchased item retains its remaining quantity.
- Restore does not create a fresh shopping need. Deliberately adding the product again uses the separate approved fresh-item behavior.
- If the previous assigned buyer is no longer a household member, restore the item as Unassigned and retain past activity in history, subject to the eventual account-deletion/privacy design.
- For concurrent restore/delete actions, deletion takes precedence. Both involved people receive in-app and push notices under the approved deletion-conflict policy. A deliberate restore after seeing that deletion still works; deletion does not permanently prevent restoration.
- Restoring an unfinished item when a matching active item exists merges them using the approved name/unit, quantity and assignment rules, preserving both histories and all distinct purchases. Example: restoring deleted Milk ×2 alongside active Milk ×3 produces one Milk ×3 under the default larger-amount rule.
- Restoring an already completed item must not change the user-facing representation when a matching fresh active demand exists. Leave that active item's requested/remaining amounts, purchase status and assignment unchanged; do not add a duplicate visible entry or count old purchases toward the new demand. Preserve the restored item's historical purchases without treating them as fulfillment of the new demand. Example: restoring yesterday's purchased Milk ×2 leaves today's Milk ×3 displayed exactly as before, still needing 3. This supersedes the proposal to show a separate restored completed entry alongside the new demand; storage/history organization remains an implementation decision.

## Approved purchase-correction conflict behavior

- Competing quantity corrections to the same existing purchase record require a manual choice. Example: one purchase recorded as 2 cartons is corrected independently to 1 and 3; do not sum these corrections or automatically select the larger quantity.
- This is distinct from independent purchases, whose quantities always add together. Preserve the original purchase and correction attribution in history.
- Until competing corrections are resolved, use the last common agreed purchase quantity for purchased totals, remaining quantities and completion, with a “Needs a choice” marker. Example: a purchase recorded as 2 and corrected concurrently to 1 and 3 temporarily counts as 2. Exact common-version identification belongs in the synchronization design.
- Any current household member may resolve a purchase-correction conflict. Both conflicting editors receive in-app and push notices. The first valid resolution accepted by the server stands; offline choices remain pending until accepted. Other resolvers see the accepted result and may subsequently correct the purchase normally. This uses the approved manual-resolution workflow.
- Undoing a purchase means that purchase was recorded incorrectly and must contribute nothing to the effective purchased total. Preserve the original record and reversal in history. Undo does not reset or overwrite the requested quantity; any changes to current demand remain in effect. Recalculate remaining demand from the current requested quantity and other valid purchases.
- Undo takes precedence over a concurrent quantity correction to the same purchase record. That purchase contributes zero; no manual choice is required. Preserve both actions in history, but do not let the correction reactivate the purchase. This supersedes the earlier manual-choice proposal for correction-versus-reversal conflicts.
- Example: requested quantity changes from 2 to 3 while an incorrect purchase of 2 is undone; with no other valid purchases, the result is 3 requested, 0 purchased and 3 remaining.
- Undo-versus-correction conflicts notify both involved editors in-app only, explaining that the purchase was undone. No push notification and no manual choice are required. This is an explicit exception to the push-enabled deletion and manual-resolution conflict notices.

## Approved item-name conflict behavior

- Conflicting renames of the same item require a manual choice. Until resolved, retain the last common name with a “Needs a choice” marker. Example: Bread renamed independently to Wholemeal bread and Rye bread temporarily remains Bread.
- Use the approved manual-resolution workflow: any current household member may resolve; both conflicting editors receive in-app and push notices; the first valid resolution accepted by the server stands. Offline resolutions remain pending until accepted.
- Reject a rename that would make an item match another active item's name and unit in the same list, using the approved capitalization/whitespace comparison. Keep the existing name; do not merge the items. This is an explicit exception to automatic merging of duplicate additions and approved restorations.
- If a name/unit collision is discovered only when an offline rename synchronizes, reject that rename, restore the last accepted name, and notify the editor in-app only with the reason. Preserve unrelated edits and purchases; do not merge the items. Concurrent collisions and a last accepted name that also collides still require protocol rules.

## Approved account requirement

- Signup/sign-in is required before production shopping-list use. The user rejected private on-device use without signup. This supersedes the anonymous-use proposal in the preserved source handover.
- Exclude guest mode and anonymous-to-account data migration from v1. This does not remove offline editing for an already authenticated user or authorize implementation yet.
- Signup verifies the user's email with an emailed one-time code; creating a password is optional and can be done later in account settings.
- Subsequent sign-in supports either email/password (if a password is set) or an emailed one-time code, accessing the same account and household memberships. Do not create separate identities for the two methods. These are alternative methods, not two-factor authentication.
- Keep users signed in on their phones across ordinary app launches so opening a shopping list does not require another code. This does not imply indefinite credentials; provider selection, secure session renewal/expiry, revocation and password setup/recovery still require technical design.
- If a previously signed-in user's session expires while offline, keep cached lists usable and save edits locally. Successful session renewal or reauthentication to the same account is required before syncing, with household membership rechecked. This does not override explicit logout or confirmed account/membership revocation. Do not claim these pending changes are remotely synchronized.
- Before explicit sign-out with unsynced edits, warn the user and allow sign-out while preserving pending work privately in account-isolated local storage. Hide account content while signed out and from other signed-in accounts. Restore access only after signing back into the same account, subject to current household membership; never attach pending edits to a different account. Storage protection and any explicit discard option remain technical/product details to resolve.
- After signup, offer the choice to create a household or join an existing household through an invitation. Detailed invitation behavior remains open.
- Any current household member may invite another person to join.
- Invitations are shareable links valid for 7 days, each usable by one person. The recipient must sign up or sign in before joining. Consume the invitation only on successful acceptance, not on opening the link. Expired, revoked, consumed or closed-household invitations cannot grant membership.
- An unused invitation may be cancelled by its inviter or the current household admin. Automatically cancel an inviter's pending invitations when they leave or are removed; this does not remove people who already joined. This is an explicit membership-administration exception to the general shared-content rule.
- Only the current household owner/admin may remove other members. The creator initially holds this role; ownership can pass to a successor as described below. Every member may leave voluntarily; other administrative permissions are not implied.
- When the owner/admin leaves and other members remain, they may select a remaining member to take over. If no successor is selected, transfer the role automatically to the first remaining member alphabetically. The successor inherits member-removal authority; historical creator attribution remains unchanged. The alphabetic name field/collation and ties require specification.
- When the last member leaves, close the household and invalidate all outstanding invitations. An old invitation must not reopen the closed household. Closure does not itself authorize immediate permanent data deletion; retention/recovery, account deletion and transfer notification details remain open.
- If a member is removed while offline, reject their not-yet-accepted household changes when they reconnect and explain that access was removed. Do not apply them to the household or claim they synchronized. Previously accepted operations remain in shared history under the existing rules. The offline phone cannot learn of removal until it reconnects.
- Once removal is confirmed, remove the household from that person's accessible lists and block access to its cached contents. Keep the access-removed explanation available separately. Physical cache cleanup and disposition of rejected unsynced work remain undecided; this approval is about access, not permanent erasure.

## Approved shopping-list organization

- Each household supports multiple named shopping lists in v1, for example “Weekly groceries” and “Party”. Lists follow the general household availability/editing rule.
- Automatic item merging is confined to the same list. Matching items in different lists remain independent demands.
- Each list offers common and grouped-by-buyer views of the same underlying items, as already required by the handover.
- Start in the common list view and remember each person's common/grouped-by-buyer preference on their phone. Switching views does not change other members' views.
- Active items appear in addition order, with newly added items at the bottom. Deterministic ordering for concurrent offline additions, merges and restorations still needs technical specification.
- Completed items move into a collapsible “Purchased” section at the bottom of each list, below active shopping items. Preserve buyer/purchase attribution and excess-quantity indicators. Items reopened by corrections or reversals return to the active area under the approved completion rules.
- In grouped-by-buyer view, show one shared Purchased section below all active buyer groups, including Unassigned. Preserve who bought each item, including distinct purchases by multiple members. Do not split completed items into separate Purchased sections per buyer.
- Purchased starts collapsed. Remember each person's expanded/collapsed choice on their phone; changing it does not change other members' views. Per-list preference storage is the proposed implementation scope.

## Approved batch — 2026-11-24

This section records the answers collected since the last file update, including decisions made on November 20, 23 and 24. Where an earlier section still lists one of these topics as open, these approved rules supersede that outdated note. This batch does not authorize implementation.

### Quantities, purchases and membership

- No selected measurement unit means whole-number quantities only. This does not equate an omitted unit with a named unit for automatic merging.
- Automatically round decimal-capable quantities to three decimal places. Do not interrupt for ordinary nonzero rounding. If a positive amount rounds to zero, ask for a larger amount or smaller unit rather than save zero. The earlier proposal to reject all excess precision is withdrawn.
- Completed items offer “Add purchase”, requiring the buyer to enter the extra quantity explicitly. Preserve it as an independent purchase under existing attribution and idempotency rules.
- Increasing requested quantity beyond the effective purchased total automatically reopens the item with the remaining amount. Example: 2 bought, demand changed to 3 means 1 remaining.
- Reducing demand below the purchased amount marks the item completed and shows the excess. Example: 2 bought, demand reduced from 3 to 1 shows “2 of 1 bought · 1 extra”.
- When a member leaves or is removed, unfinished items assigned to them become Unassigned. Preserve their historical purchases/attribution subject to the eventual account-deletion design.

### Measurement units and catalog retirement

- Block changing a custom unit from decimal-capable to whole-number-only while fractional quantities already use it; do not invalidate or round existing records to permit the change.
- If an offline unit change conflicts with a purchase recorded in the original unit, preserve that purchase and keep the original unit. Notify the unit editor in-app that the change could not be applied.
- If all purchases on an item have been undone, changing its unit becomes allowed again. Historical purchase records retain their original units.
- Independently created custom units merge automatically when their names and whole-number/decimal settings match. Exact name normalization and canonical identity reconciliation require technical specification.
- Custom-unit creation also checks for similar existing names, offering “Use existing unit” or “Create separately”. Similarity alone does not merge units. Choosing an existing unit adopts that unit's existing quantity rules.
- Same-name units with different whole-number/decimal settings require a manual choice. Allow decimals temporarily so existing quantities remain valid. Whole-number-only remains unavailable while fractional quantities use the unit.
- For these unit conflicts, any household member may resolve; both involved creators receive in-app and push notices; the first valid resolution accepted by the server stands.
- Conflicting renames of the same custom unit keep its previous name until resolved through the same manual-choice workflow, including notices to both editors.
- Block a unit rename that duplicates another existing unit name and offer “Use existing unit”. If chosen while editing an item, switch only that item's unit; do not replace the unit on other items. The restriction on changing units with effective purchases still applies. A replacement-everywhere feature is not approved. Behavior in standalone unit management without an item context still needs design.
- If a unit is retired while another member uses it offline, preserve that entry and its unit. After synchronization the retired unit is unavailable for new selections.
- Apply the same retirement rule to catalog products: preserve list entries added offline, while hiding the retired product from future catalog selections.

### Household conflict-setting changes

- Changing the household quantity-conflict rule affects new conflicts only. Do not recalculate resolved quantities.
- Conflicts already awaiting manual resolution remain pending under their original rule.
- If members change the setting differently offline, the last valid setting change accepted by the server becomes the shared setting; notify both editors in-app. This acceptance ordering is specific to setting changes, not a general rule for item edits.
- Newly discovered conflicts use the current household rule even when the underlying edits were made offline under an older rule. Exact policy version binding requires technical specification.

### Notification behavior

- Group notifications when multiple conflicts arrive together after syncing, for example “3 items need your attention”, retaining individual details in the app. Preserve each conflict's previously approved recipients and channels: do not turn in-app-only conflicts into push alerts or reveal another member's conflicts through grouping.
- Unresolved conflicts stay in “Needs attention” until resolved, even after their notifications are opened/read.
- Opening an informational notice that needs no decision marks it read while retaining it in notification history. Reading a notice is not conflict resolution. History retention duration remains open.

## Approved batch — list and account deletion

Collected after the preceding batch; these decisions supersede earlier notes describing the same behavior as open. Implementation remains unauthorized.

### Recoverable list deletion

- Deleting a whole list moves it to Deleted lists, preserving its items and purchase history for restoration. Any current household member may restore it under the general collaborative rule.
- If whole-list deletion conflicts with an offline purchase, keep the list visible and preserve the purchase. Notify both involved people through the established deletion-conflict channels (in-app and push).
- If deletion conflicts only with offline item additions or edits, keep the list in Deleted lists while retaining those changes for restoration. Notify both involved people.
- Deletion wins over a concurrent list restoration, with notifications to both people. A deliberate subsequent restoration still works. The brief earlier choice of restoration winning was explicitly superseded by the user's later “Deletion wins” instruction; both item and list rules now agree.
- Deleted lists remain recoverable until explicit permanent deletion, not automatic expiry.

### Permanent list deletion

- Delete permanently requires confirmation explaining that the list, its items and purchase history cannot be restored. Reusable household catalog products remain available.
- The general household-content permissions apply unless explicitly restricted; no new admin-only restriction was requested.
- Permanent deletion overrides pending offline changes to that list. On reconnection, discard those changes and show an in-app explanation. They must not recreate the permanently deleted list.
- Technical deletion bookkeeping, backups and retention must be designed to enforce this behavior without promising unverified instantaneous erasure from every storage layer.

### Account deletion

- Deleting an account preserves shared household lists and purchases for remaining members. Replace that user's historical display attribution with “Deleted member”. Personal-data removal and retained shared-record structure require technical/privacy design; this product behavior is not a claim of legal compliance.
- If the departing account owns a household, allow a chosen successor; otherwise transfer administration alphabetically among remaining members. If nobody remains, close the household and apply the permanent-deletion behavior approved in the following batch.
- Require a fresh emailed verification code and final confirmation before account deletion proceeds.
- A later signup with the same email creates a new user identity. Do not automatically restore old household memberships, ownership or historical attribution, or attach the deleted account's pending local work. The user must join/create households anew.

## Approved batch — account security and onboarding

These decisions supersede earlier open notes on the same subjects. They do not authorize implementation.

### Last-member departure

- When the last member leaves, warn them beforehand that the household closes and its remaining lists and history are permanently deleted.
- Apply the same warning and deletion when the last member deletes their account. Do not delete shared households that still have other members; use the approved ownership-transfer and Deleted member rules there.
- Outstanding invitations become invalid under the existing closure rule. Technical cleanup, backup handling and deletion bookkeeping remain to be specified; the user-facing behavior has no recovery period.

### Account security

- Recover a forgotten password by signing in with an emailed one-time code and setting a new password in account settings.
- Changing or resetting a password signs out other devices while keeping the current device signed in.
- Account settings also provide “Sign out all other devices” without requiring a password change.
- Changing the email address requires a fresh code sent to the current address and verification of the new address. Preserve the same account identity and household memberships. Lost access to the old mailbox is not covered by this approved flow.
- Revocation timing for offline devices and protected handling of their pending work still need a technical design; do not imply an offline phone instantly learns of server-side revocation.

### Household initialization and invitations

- On household creation, ask whether to initialize an empty product catalog or a starter set of common groceries. Joining users use the household's existing catalog rather than initializing another one.
- The starter set populates only the catalog, not shopping demand. Starter contents/localization remain implementation planning decisions.
- Create one empty, renameable list named “Shopping list” when a household is created.
- Preserve an invitation through signup/sign-in and return the user to it without making them reopen the link. Revalidate it after authentication.
- Before acceptance, show the household name and inviter with an explicit “Join household” button. Authentication alone does not join the household or consume its invitation.

### Display names

- Signup asks for a display name, used for assignments, purchase attribution and alphabetical admin succession.
- Members may change their display name later. Existing attribution shows the updated name while staying linked to the same stable person/account identity. The existing account-deletion rule still replaces attribution with “Deleted member”.
- Household name uniqueness and collision behavior are approved in the following batch. Exact Unicode normalization, validation and sorting collation still require technical specification.

## Approved batch — names, catalog edits and online actions

These decisions supersede earlier proposals/open notes on the same subjects. Implementation remains unauthorized.

### Household display names

- Display names must be unique within each household. Compare names ignoring capitalization and extra spaces; “Alex”, “alex” and “ Alex ” collide. The previous proposal to permit duplicate names is rejected.
- If a joining user's name is already taken, require a household-specific name without changing their name in other households. Keep the same stable account identity.
- Block a later name change to another member's existing name and ask for another name.
- If two members choose the same available name offline, the first valid change accepted by the server keeps it. The other member retains their previous name and receives an in-app request to choose another.

### Online-only operations

- Changing email, resetting passwords, deleting accounts and managing household membership require an internet connection. Shopping-list work remains available offline.
- This does not require online access for household display-name edits: the explicitly approved offline name-collision rule above still applies. Precise authorization, revocation and retry handling belong in technical design.

### Shared catalog renaming

- Renaming a catalog product updates its active shopping-list entries across that household. Past purchase history retains the product name recorded when purchased.
- Renaming a product from a shopping list edits the shared catalog product too; it is not a list-local name override.
- Block a rename if the new name already belongs to another product in the household catalog. This adds catalog-level uniqueness validation alongside the existing active-item collision rules. Exact catalog identity/unit modeling and retired-name collision handling still need specification.
- Retain the previously approved manual-choice workflow for competing renames and in-app explanation for rejected offline collisions. Propagation must preserve quantities, assignment and purchase facts.

### Shopping-list names

- Active shopping-list names are unique within a household, ignoring capitalization and extra spaces. Deleted lists do not permanently reserve names.
- If someone enters the name of a list in Deleted lists, offer restoration. They may decline and create a new list with that name while the old list remains deleted. Later restoring the old list requires a different name if the name is now in use. See the following batch for offline collisions.

## Approved batch — retired names and offline duplicate detection

These decisions supersede earlier open notes/proposals on the same subjects. Implementation remains unauthorized.

### Name reuse and restoration

- A deleted list's name may be reused for a new list after declining restoration. Preserve the old deleted list independently. If restored later while that name is occupied, require a different name.
- Apply the same rule to retired catalog products: declining restoration permits a new product with that name, leaving the retired product and its history intact. Restoring the old product requires a different name when its former name is in use.
- Retired custom units follow the same name-reuse and rename-on-restoration rules. These are new identities, not silent replacement or reassignment of old history/references.

### Concurrent list creation

- Preserve both lists when members independently create same-name lists offline. The first valid list accepted by the server keeps its name; ask the other creator to rename their list.
- Keep the second list and its contents accessible under a temporary label such as “Shopping list (rename needed)”. Treat this as a pending-name presentation state, not permission to discard contents or merge lists. Exact collision bookkeeping remains technical design work.
- Notify the affected creator in-app only, without push.

### Similar products created offline

- On synchronization, flag similar but non-identical independently created products for “Merge” or “Keep separate”. Do not automatically merge on similarity alone. Exact-match merging follows the already approved rules.
- Any current household member can decide. Both creators receive in-app and push notices under the approved grouping rules; the first valid decision accepted by the server stands.
- Remember a “Keep separate” decision for that product pair, and suppress future duplicate suggestions for the pair unless one product is renamed. Do not infer that the decision disables similarity checks against other products.
- User-facing choices for different units/defaults are approved in the following batch; formal identity and reconciliation behavior still require technical specification. Preserve distinct purchases and history under the existing invariants.

## Approved batch — merging products, units and notes

These decisions supersede earlier proposals/open notes on the same subjects. Implementation remains unauthorized.

### Product merges

- The person confirming a merge chooses which existing product name to keep.
- If the products have different usual units, choose the default unit for future additions. Existing list entries and purchases retain their own units.
- Entries for the merged products in the same list stay separate when their units differ, for example 2 litres and 1 carton. Matching-unit entries follow existing quantity/assignment merge rules. Never convert or sum unlike measures implicitly.

### Similar custom-unit decisions

- Do not remember “Keep separate” as a lasting suppression rule for custom-unit pairs. Future creation attempts may show similarity suggestions again. This differs from the approved remembered product-pair decisions; it does not authorize repeatedly reopening the same resolved conflict on every sync.
- Similar units created independently offline are flagged on synchronization for “Use one unit” or “Keep separate”; similarity alone never causes automatic merging.
- Any current household member can resolve. Both creators receive in-app and push notices, subject to grouping; the first valid resolution accepted by the server stands.
- On Use one unit, choose which existing unit survives. A whole-number-only unit is unavailable if fractional quantities are recorded against either unit.
- Combining units updates existing list entries and product defaults to use the chosen unit. Preserve original unit names in purchase history. This explicit household-wide combination is separate from the earlier item-local Use existing unit action offered during a blocked rename.
- Only combine units representing the same measure and scale, for example litre/liter. Quantities remain unchanged. Do not combine grams with kilograms or silently perform conversions. How custom-unit equivalence is established must be specified; similar names alone are not proof of equivalence.

### Shopping-list entry notes

- A shopping-list entry may have an optional note, for example “ripe bananas” or “unsweetened”. This is an entry-specific feature; automatic reuse as a catalog default is not approved.
- When duplicate entries merge, preserve distinct notes and avoid repeating identical notes. Approved example: Bananas with “ripe” and Bananas with “small ones” combine into an entry retaining both, such as “ripe; small ones”. Storage representation and note equality still need technical specification.
- Final note decisions saved in the 2026-11-25 consolidation: competing offline edits to the same note preserve both text versions without a manual choice, with an in-app notice to both editors and no push. If clearing a note conflicts with editing it, keep the edited text and notify both editors in-app only.
